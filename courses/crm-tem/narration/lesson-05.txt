Domestic Airlines — CRM/TEM — Initial and Recurrent
Lesson 5: Errors and Error Management

Learning objectives:
- Define a flight crew error.
- Classify errors: aircraft handling, procedural, communication.
- Describe error detection and response.

Definition

Errors are "actions or inactions by the flight crew that lead to deviations from organisational or flight crew intentions or expectations". They reduce safety margins and increase the probability of adverse events. Errors can be spontaneous, linked to a threat, or part of an error chain.

Three categories — by the primary interaction

Tap a card to turn it over.
Aircraft handling error the crew interacts with the aircraft Manual flying (vertical, lateral, speed deviations, flaps, speedbrakes, power); automation (wrong altitude, speed, heading, mode, entries); systems (packs, anti-ice, altimeter, fuel, speed bugs, frequency); ground navigation (wrong taxiway, too fast, failure to hold short). Procedural error the crew interacts with a procedure SOPs (no cross-verification of automation inputs); checklists (missed items, wrong time); callouts omitted; briefings omitted; documentation (wrong weight and balance, fuel, ATIS, clearance, logbook, MEL). Communication error the crew interacts with people Crew to external: missed calls, misinterpreted instructions, wrong read-back, wrong clearance/taxiway/runway. Pilot to pilot: miscommunication or misinterpretation. Intentional non-compliance a sub-set Deliberate deviation from SOPs or regulations — inside any of the three categories, not a separate one. Proficiency a sub-set Skill or knowledge deficiencies, training deficiencies — underlying any of the three categories.

Error management

What matters is whether the error is detected , by whom, how the crew responds , and the outcome . Predictive monitoring identifies threats; reactive monitoring identifies errors. Systemic countermeasures (TCAS, GPWS, windshear warning, SOPs, checklists, briefings) and human-skill countermeasures (knowledge, monitoring, teamwork, workload management) resist and resolve errors.

Key takeaways:
- Errors come from the crew: actions or inactions leading to deviations.
- Handling, procedural, communication — by the primary interaction.
- Non-compliance and proficiency are sub-sets, not categories.
- Detect, respond, check the outcome.
