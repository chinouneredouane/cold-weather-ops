Lesson 05: Planning, CRM and Lessons Learned

Lesson narration
Checking for narration…

Learning objectives

  • Integrate windshear and thunderstorm threats into planning and briefings.
  • Apply decision points and CRM.
  • Learn from a classic windshear accident.

Planning and briefing

  • Brief convective weather on the route and at destination, alternates, extra fuel for deviations and holding.
  • Before take-off or approach in convective weather: brief the windshear escape, the go-around and the decision points (“if windshear is reported on final, we hold / divert”).
  • Agree on the callouts for speed and vertical speed deviations.

CRM

  • Plan continuation bias is a strong factor in convective weather — “the aircraft ahead landed”. Each aircraft meets a different part of the storm.
  • Either pilot can call for the escape manoeuvre; the PF executes immediately.
  • Use ATC and other crews' reports, and give your own.

Lesson learned — Delta Air Lines 191 (Dallas/Fort Worth, August 1985)

A Lockheed L-1011 on approach flew into a microburst from a thunderstorm close to the runway. The aircraft lost airspeed and height and struck the ground short of the runway; 137 people died. The investigation led directly to windshear training programmes and to the development of airborne windshear detection systems.

Key message

Do not penetrate a thunderstorm or approach through one. Waiting 20 minutes for a storm to pass costs fuel — never safety.

Key takeaways

  • Brief convective threats, the escape manoeuvre and decision points.
  • Beware of plan continuation — each aircraft meets a different storm.
  • Delta 191 (1985) shaped modern windshear training and detection systems.
