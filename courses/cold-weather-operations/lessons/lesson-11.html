Lesson 11: Gate and Remote De-icing Operations

Lesson 11 of 30≈ 7 min⬇ Download PDF
Lesson narration

Learning objectives

  • Compare gate de-icing with remote (pad) de-icing.
  • Describe a typical remote de-icing pad procedure.
  • Manage the time between treatment and take-off.

Two ways to de-ice

At the gate / standRemote pad or central facility
EnginesNormally shut down; APU runningNormally running at idle (per AFM and facility procedure)
Distance to runwayLonger taxi — more holdover time used before take-offClose to the departure runway — less holdover time used
CommunicationGround crew via interphone or radioPad control / de-icing coordinator by radio, then the vehicle operator
Main risksHOT running out during pushback and taxiWrong position on the pad, engines near personnel, confusion between ATC, pad control and the de-icing crew

A typical remote pad procedure

  1. Before taxi, check the airport de-icing procedures (charts, NOTAMs, frequencies) and brief the sequence.
  2. ATC or the coordinator assigns a pad. Taxi in following the lead-in line and marshaller or stop-position signs, and stop on the stop line.
  3. Set the parking brake and configure the aircraft for de-icing. Report "configured" on the pad frequency.
  4. Agree the treatment and receive the start call. Hold position — no movement, power or configuration change.
  5. Receive the anti-icing code and the "all personnel and equipment clear" call.
  6. Only then reconfigure, complete the after de-icing checks, and contact ATC to leave the pad as instructed.

Managing the time to take-off

  • Coordinate with ATC before treatment so that you are not de-iced and then held for a long time.
  • Taxi at a safe speed with good separation. Avoid the jet blast of the aircraft ahead, which blows snow and slush onto treated surfaces and thins the fluid.
  • Some AFMs recommend delaying flap extension until close to the runway when taxiing through slush or snow. Others require flaps to be set before anti-icing so that the surfaces are protected. Follow your FCOM.
  • Continuously compare the remaining holdover time with the expected take-off time. If it will not be enough, decide early — a planned return for re-treatment is better than a rushed decision at the holding point.

Key takeaways

  • Gate de-icing uses more holdover time; pad de-icing is closer to the runway but more complex.
  • On the pad, stay stationary and configured until “all clear” is received.
  • Keep clear of jet blast and compare remaining HOT with the expected take-off time.
