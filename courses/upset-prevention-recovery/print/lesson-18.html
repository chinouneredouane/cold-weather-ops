Lesson 18: Unreliable Airspeed and Instrument Failures

Lesson 18 of 24 · about 8 min

Learning objectives

  • Recognise the symptoms of unreliable airspeed.
  • Apply the generic memory actions: pitch and thrust.
  • Describe how to troubleshoot and stabilise after unreliable air data.

Causes

  • Pitot probes blocked by ice crystals, heavy rain, volcanic ash, insects or covers left in place.
  • Static ports blocked — for example by tape or residue after maintenance or washing.
  • Failed probe heaters, damaged probes, or faulty air data computers.

Symptoms

  • Disagreement between the captain's, first officer's and standby airspeed or altitude.
  • Speed that does not match the pitch and thrust (for example, speed rising in a climb at constant thrust).
  • Simultaneous, contradictory warnings: overspeed and stall warning together, or "rudder ratio", "Mach trim" and altitude alerts.
  • Autopilot and autothrust disconnect, flight control law degradation on fly-by-wire types.

Memory actions

Most manufacturers' unreliable airspeed procedures begin with memory items along these lines:

  1. Autopilot and autothrust — disconnect (they may be using the faulty data).
  2. Flight directors — off where the procedure requires it.
  3. Pitch and thrust — set to the memory values for the flight phase (for example, a climb attitude and climb thrust after take-off; a level-flight attitude at cruise).

Use the exact values and procedure from your QRH. Do not chase the airspeed indication.

Stabilise, then troubleshoot

  • When safe — above minimum safe altitude, wings level, stable pitch and thrust — use the QRH tables to set pitch and thrust for the phase of flight and weight.
  • Use other information: GPS ground speed, angle of attack indication where fitted, the flight path vector, and ATC for ground speed and altitude (in an emergency).
  • Identify a reliable source if possible. If none, continue with pitch and power tables.
Key lesson

In several accidents, a short air data problem became a fatal stall because the crew reacted to the false indications with large, sustained pitch inputs. Pitch and power first, avoid large inputs, and respect the stall warning.

Key takeaways

  • Unreliable airspeed: disagreements, impossible combinations, contradictory warnings.
  • Memory items: autopilot and autothrust off, flight directors off as required, set memory pitch and thrust.
  • Stabilise first, then troubleshoot with QRH tables and other data.

Knowledge check

Shortly after take-off, the airspeed indications disagree and both overspeed and stall warnings occur. What is the first action?

  1. Follow the flight director
  2. Apply the unreliable airspeed memory items: autopilot and autothrust off, set memory pitch and thrust
  3. Pull up to reduce the overspeed
  4. Reduce thrust to idle

Answer: B. Contradictory warnings indicate unreliable air data. Disconnect the automation and fly the memory pitch and thrust.
