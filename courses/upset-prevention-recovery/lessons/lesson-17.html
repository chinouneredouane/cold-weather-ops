Lesson 17: Recognising a Developing Upset

Lesson 17 of 24≈ 8 min⬇ Download PDF
Lesson narration
Checking for narration…

Learning objectives

  • Identify the flight-deck cues of a developing upset.
  • Read an attitude indicator quickly and correctly in an unusual attitude.
  • Confirm the situation by cross-checking instruments.

Cues of a developing upset

  • Speed: trend vector towards the low-speed band or the overspeed limit; speed not stabilising after a thrust change.
  • Attitude: pitch or bank increasing without an intended input; pitch limit indicator approaching the aircraft symbol.
  • Energy: thrust at idle when it should be climbing; pitch high and speed decaying.
  • Automation: unexpected mode changes, autopilot or autothrust disconnect, flight control law changes.
  • Physical: buffet, unusual noise, unexpected control forces, g changes.
  • Warnings: stall warning, overspeed, bank angle aural alert, windshear alert.

Reading the attitude indicator

  • Look at the attitude indicator first. Determine pitch (sky or ground? which pitch line?) and bank (where is the sky pointer?).
  • The sky pointer (or bank pointer) always points towards the sky. In a nose-low, high-bank upset, roll towards the sky pointer to bring the wings level.
  • At extreme attitudes, the display may show mostly sky or mostly ground, and chevrons may point the way back to the horizon. Know your PFD.

Confirm before you act

  • Cross-check both primary attitude indicators and the standby instrument. A single failed indicator can create a false upset.
  • Confirm the trend with airspeed, altitude and vertical speed. A nose-high attitude with decaying speed and a climbing altimeter is very different from a nose-high attitude with a rapid descent (possible stall).
  • Say what you see: “Nose high, wings level, speed decreasing.” This helps both pilots build the same picture.
Act early

If you recognise a developing upset, correct it immediately with small, smooth inputs. A small correction now prevents a large recovery later.

Key takeaways

  • Developing upsets show up as speed, attitude, energy, automation, physical and warning cues.
  • Read the attitude indicator first; the sky pointer always points to the sky.
  • Confirm with the other attitude indicator, the standby instrument and the speed, altitude and vertical speed trends.
