Domestic Airlines — Upset Prevention and Recovery
Lesson 11: Weight and Balance Effects

Learning objectives:
- Explain how weight affects stall speed, performance and buffet margin.
- Describe the effects of forward and aft centre of gravity on stability and control.
- Recognise how loading errors can contribute to an upset.

Weight

Stall speed increases with the square root of weight. A heavier aircraft must fly faster for the same margin.

At high altitude, higher weight reduces the buffet margin and the maximum altitude (Lesson 7).

Climb performance and acceleration are lower at high weight — less energy is available in a recovery.

An incorrect weight entered in the FMS produces incorrect speeds, which can erode the stall margin from take-off onwards.

Centre of gravity

Forward CG Aft CG

Longitudinal stability Greater Reduced

Pitch control forces Heavier Lighter — easy to over-control

Stall speed Slightly higher Slightly lower

Elevator authority More needed to rotate and flare Less nose-down authority for stall recovery

Fuel consumption Slightly higher (more tail down-force) Slightly lower

Loading errors

Cargo or passengers loaded differently from the load sheet can move the CG outside the envelope.

Unsecured cargo that shifts during rotation or in turbulence can cause a sudden, large pitch change.

Incorrect trim settings for take-off — from a wrong CG — can cause an early or late rotation and a nose-high or tail-strike situation.

Prevention Check the load sheet and the FMS entries carefully, cross-check weight and CG with the fuel and passenger figures, and be alert to unusual control forces at rotation. If the aircraft does not feel right, say so.

Key takeaways:
- Higher weight: higher stall speed, lower buffet margin, less excess energy.
- Aft CG: less stable, lighter forces, less nose-down authority. Forward CG: heavier forces, higher stall speed.
- Loading and FMS weight errors erode margins before the flight begins.
