ICAO and the Chicago Convention

The Convention on International Civil Aviation (Chicago, 1944) created the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO). Contracting States agree to apply ICAO Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs), published in the Annexes, and to notify ICAO of any differences (published in the AIP).

  • Standards — “shall”: necessary for safety or regularity; States must comply or notify a difference.
  • Recommended Practices — “should”: desirable; States endeavour to comply.
  • PANS (e.g. PANS-OPS Doc 8168, PANS-ATM Doc 4444) — procedures with a wider level of detail.
  • Manuals and circulars — guidance material (e.g. Doc 9613 PBN, Doc 9574 RVSM, Doc 10011 UPRT).

Key takeaways

  • The Chicago Convention created ICAO; States apply SARPs or notify differences.
  • Standards (“shall”), Recommended Practices (“should”), PANS and guidance manuals.
  • Annex 6 Part I is the core operational Annex for commercial air transport.

Knowledge check

What must a State do if it cannot comply with an ICAO Standard?

  1. Nothing
  2. Notify ICAO of the difference (published in the AIP)
  3. Leave ICAO
  4. Ask the operator to decide

Answer: B. Differences from Standards must be notified to ICAO.
