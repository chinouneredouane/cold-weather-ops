Domestic Airlines — Operations Manual and Regulations
Lesson 3: The Operations Manual

Learning objectives:
- Describe the structure of the Operations Manual.
- Find information quickly in each part.
- Apply the rules on amendments and availability.

Structure (AMC1 ORO.MLR.100)

Part Content

OM A — General / Basic All non-type-related policies and procedures: organisation, responsibilities, crew composition, flight time limitations, operating procedures, dangerous goods, security, accident and incident handling, rules of the air.

OM B — Aircraft operating matters Type-related: limitations, normal, abnormal and emergency procedures, performance, flight planning, mass and balance, loading, MEL, systems.

OM C — Route and aerodrome Route and aerodrome instructions and information: minimum altitudes, aerodrome operating minima, communications, navigation facilities, special procedures, aerodrome categories.

OM D — Training Training programmes and checking for all operations personnel.

Using the manual

The Operations Manual must be kept up to date ; the crew is responsible for using the current revision.

Relevant parts must be available on board (paper or approved electronic format).

Read and acknowledge every amendment, temporary revision and operations notice as the company requires.

If the Operations Manual and the manufacturer's documents differ, follow the company's rule on precedence, normally stated in OM A.

Company documents This lesson explains the international framework. The exact company rules are in the Domestic Airlines Operations Manual and the national regulations of the DACM — always refer to the current edition.

Key takeaways:
- OM A general, OM B aircraft, OM C route and aerodrome, OM D training.
- Use the current revision; acknowledge amendments and notices.
- Know where to find information under time pressure.
