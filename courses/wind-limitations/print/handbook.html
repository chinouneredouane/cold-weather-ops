Module 1 · Wind and Limitations

Lesson 01: Wind Reports and Wind Components

Lesson 1 of 3 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Interpret wind reports correctly.
  • Calculate headwind, tailwind and crosswind components.
  • Use gusts correctly.

Wind reports

  • In the METAR, the wind is a 10-minute mean; in reports for take-off and landing from the tower (and ATIS), it is a 2-minute mean for the runway (ICAO Annex 3).
  • Gusts are reported when the maximum wind exceeds the mean by 10 kt or more: 27015G28KT = 270°, 15 kt, gusting 28 kt.
  • Variable direction: 27015KT 240V300 — direction varies between 240° and 300°.
  • Directions in METAR / TAF are true; tower and ATIS winds are magnetic.

Components

  • Use the FMS / EFB, the wind component chart or the rule of thumb.
  • For limits, check the crosswind with the gust and the tailwind with the steady wind and the gust as your procedure states.
  • With a variable wind direction, use the most unfavourable direction.

Key takeaways

  • METAR wind: 10-min mean, true; tower / ATIS: 2-min mean, magnetic.
  • Gusts reported when 10 kt or more above the mean.
  • Crosswind ≈ wind × sin(angle); use the gust for crosswind limits.

Knowledge check

Wind 090°/20 kt on runway 06. Approximately what is the crosswind?

  1. 5 kt
  2. 10 kt
  3. 17 kt
  4. 20 kt

Answer: B. 30° off the runway: half the wind, about 10 kt.

Lesson 02: Aircraft Wind Limitations

Lesson 2 of 3 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish demonstrated crosswind from a limitation.
  • Apply tailwind limits.
  • Use the manufacturer's crosswind guidance by runway condition.

Demonstrated crosswind

The AFM states the maximum demonstrated crosswind — the highest crosswind tested during certification. Unless the AFM says it is a limitation, it is not a structural or legal limit — but the company normally turns it, or a lower value, into an operational limit in the OM B.

Tailwind

The AFM gives a maximum tailwind for take-off and landing (commonly 10 kt; some aircraft are approved for more with specific conditions). This is a limitation. Performance must also be calculated with the reported tailwind.

Crosswind and runway condition

  • Manufacturers publish recommended maximum crosswinds that decrease as the runway condition worsens — by runway condition code (RWYCC) or braking action.
  • On a contaminated runway, use the crosswind for the reported RWYCC (see the Cold Weather Operations course).

Other wind-related limits

  • Autoland and low-visibility operations have their own headwind, tailwind and crosswind limits.
  • Doors: maximum wind for opening or closing passenger and cargo doors.
  • Engine start in strong tailwind or crosswind (especially for some engines and propellers).
Company limits

The actual Domestic Airlines wind limits for each fleet — crosswind by runway condition, tailwind, gusts, limits for the first officer as pilot flying and for low-visibility / autoland — are in the OM B, the FCOM / AOM and the AFM. Always use them; the figures in this course are examples only.

Key takeaways

  • Demonstrated crosswind: tested, not normally a limitation — but the company may make it one.
  • Tailwind limit in the AFM is a limitation.
  • Crosswind limits reduce with the runway condition; autoland has its own limits.

Knowledge check

The AFM maximum demonstrated crosswind is:

  1. Always a structural limit
  2. The highest crosswind tested in certification — not a limitation unless the AFM says so
  3. The same as the tailwind limit
  4. Only for take-off

Answer: B. It is a demonstrated value, not a limitation unless stated.

Lesson 03: Company Limits and Crew Application

Lesson 3 of 3 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Apply company wind limits for each fleet and crew situation.
  • Use speed additives and technique for wind.
  • Decide when the wind does not allow take-off or landing.

Company limits

The OM B normally defines, for each fleet:

  • maximum crosswind for take-off and landing — dry, wet and by RWYCC — including gusts;
  • maximum tailwind;
  • more restrictive limits when the first officer is pilot flying or for crews with limited experience on type;
  • limits for low-visibility operations and autoland;
  • possible restrictions at specific aerodromes (OM C).

Application

  1. Get the latest wind (ATIS / tower) and the runway condition.
  2. Calculate the components with the gust; check the most restrictive limit that applies (aircraft, company, crew, runway condition, LVO).
  3. Apply the approach speed wind additive defined in your FCOM / AOM.
  4. Brief the crosswind technique and the go-around if the limits are exceeded on short final.
Decision

If the wind exceeds a limit, do not take off or land — wait, use another runway or divert. Wind limits are not targets.

Company limits

The actual Domestic Airlines wind limits for each fleet — crosswind by runway condition, tailwind, gusts, limits for the first officer as pilot flying and for low-visibility / autoland — are in the OM B, the FCOM / AOM and the AFM. Always use them; the figures in this course are examples only.

Key takeaways

  • Use the most restrictive limit: aircraft, company, crew, runway condition, LVO, aerodrome.
  • Calculate with the gust; apply the FCOM / AOM speed additive.
  • Above a limit: wait, change runway or divert.

Knowledge check

Which crosswind limit applies when the first officer is pilot flying on a wet runway?

  1. Always the AFM demonstrated value
  2. The most restrictive applicable limit in the OM B
  3. The tailwind limit
  4. No limit

Answer: B. Apply the most restrictive company limit that applies to the situation.
