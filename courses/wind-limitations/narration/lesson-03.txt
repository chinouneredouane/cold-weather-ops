Domestic Airlines — Wind Limitations
Lesson 3: Company Limits and Crew Application

Learning objectives:
- Apply company wind limits for each fleet and crew situation.
- Use speed additives and technique for wind.
- Decide when the wind does not allow take-off or landing.

Company limits

The OM B normally defines, for each fleet:

maximum crosswind for take-off and landing — dry, wet and by RWYCC — including gusts;

maximum tailwind;

more restrictive limits when the first officer is pilot flying or for crews with limited experience on type;

limits for low-visibility operations and autoland;

possible restrictions at specific aerodromes (OM C).

Application

Get the latest wind (ATIS / tower) and the runway condition.

Calculate the components with the gust; check the most restrictive limit that applies (aircraft, company, crew, runway condition, LVO).

Apply the approach speed wind additive defined in your FCOM / AOM.

Brief the crosswind technique and the go-around if the limits are exceeded on short final.

Decision If the wind exceeds a limit, do not take off or land — wait, use another runway or divert. Wind limits are not targets.

Company limits The actual Domestic Airlines wind limits for each fleet — crosswind by runway condition, tailwind, gusts, limits for the first officer as pilot flying and for low-visibility / autoland — are in the OM B , the FCOM / AOM and the AFM . Always use them; the figures in this course are examples only.

Key takeaways:
- Use the most restrictive limit: aircraft, company, crew, runway condition, LVO, aerodrome.
- Calculate with the gust; apply the FCOM / AOM speed additive.
- Above a limit: wait, change runway or divert.
