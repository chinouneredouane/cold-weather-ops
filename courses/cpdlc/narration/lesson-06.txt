Domestic Airlines — CPDLC — Controller-Pilot Data Link
Lesson 6: Good Practice and Common Errors

Learning objectives:
- Apply crew procedures that prevent CPDLC errors.
- Recognise common CPDLC errors.
- Manage workload when using CPDLC.

Crew procedures

Read the whole message — aloud or silently, as the SOP requires. Conditional clauses (“AT … CLIMB TO …”, “AFTER PASSING …”) are easy to miss.

Cross-check it with the other pilot before responding.

Respond — then set the clearance in the FCU/MCP or FMS, and cross-check the result.

If anything is unclear, revert to voice .

Common errors

Executing a message before responding, or responding WILCO without both pilots reading it.

Missing a condition in a conditional clearance.

Responding to the wrong message when several are pending.

Logon failures caused by wrong flight plan or FMS data.

Heads-down work with CPDLC at a critical moment — only one pilot should be heads-down at any time.

Workload CPDLC reduces frequency congestion, but it can increase heads-down time. Plan its use for cruise and for non-urgent exchanges, and keep the pilot flying focused on the flight path.

Key takeaways:
- Read in full, cross-check, respond, then act — and verify the result.
- Watch for conditional clauses and multiple pending messages.
- Only one pilot heads-down; revert to voice when in doubt.
