Domestic Airlines — CPDLC — Controller-Pilot Data Link
Lesson 4: Reverting to Voice, Phraseology and Failures

Learning objectives:
- Recognise when to revert from CPDLC to voice.
- Use CPDLC-related voice phraseology.
- Handle message failures and CPDLC system failures.

When to revert to voice

To clarify an unexpected, inappropriate or ambiguous CPDLC message.

To make sure an instruction is executed in time.

To correct an unintended message that has been sent.

When a time-out or an error occurs for a CPDLC message.

Always for urgent or emergency situations.

Phraseology

Situation Phraseology

Correcting a CPDLC message by voice “(call sign) DISREGARD CPDLC (message content or type) MESSAGE, BREAK, (corrected message)” Example: “DOMESTIC 3012, DISREGARD CPDLC CLIMB MESSAGE, BREAK, CLIMB FLIGHT LEVEL 360.”

Failure of a single message Confirm by voice the action that will be taken, prefaced with “CPDLC MESSAGE FAILURE” — or resend the message by CPDLC.

CPDLC has failed Revert to voice and inform the other party: “CPDLC FAILURE”.

Complete ground system failure Controller: “ALL STATIONS CPDLC FAILURE (unit name)”.

Stop sending requests “((call sign) or ALL STATIONS) STOP SENDING CPDLC REQUESTS [UNTIL ADVISED] [(reason)]”

Resume normal use “((call sign) or ALL STATIONS) RESUME NORMAL CPDLC OPERATIONS”

Good practice Keep listening to the voice frequency at all times while using CPDLC. A clearance received by voice takes precedence over a pending CPDLC message on the same subject — clarify if in doubt.

Key takeaways:
- Revert to voice to clarify, to ensure timely execution, to correct, after a time-out or error, and always in an emergency.
- Use “DISREGARD CPDLC … MESSAGE, BREAK …”, “CPDLC MESSAGE FAILURE”, “CPDLC FAILURE”.
- Keep a listening watch on voice.
