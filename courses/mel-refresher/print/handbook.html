Module 1 · MEL Principles

Lesson 01: MMEL, MEL and CDL

Lesson 1 of 4 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Explain the purpose of the MMEL and the MEL.
  • Describe how the MEL is produced and approved.
  • Distinguish the MEL from the CDL.

Why a MEL?

A transport aeroplane has redundant systems. The Minimum Equipment List (MEL) allows the operator to dispatch an aircraft with certain equipment inoperative, for a limited time and under defined conditions, while keeping an acceptable level of safety (ICAO Annex 6 Part I; EASA ORO.MLR.105).

  • The MMEL (Master MEL) is produced by the aircraft manufacturer and approved by the State of design (for EASA, as part of the operational suitability data).
  • The operator's MEL is based on the MMEL, adapted to its aircraft configuration, operations and procedures, and approved by its authority. It must not be less restrictive than the MMEL.
  • Equipment not listed in the MEL that is required for the flight must be operative — otherwise no dispatch (unless a specific procedure applies).

CDL

The Configuration Deviation List (CDL), part of the AFM, allows dispatch with certain external parts missing (for example a panel, fairing or static discharger), usually with performance penalties.

When the MEL applies

The MEL applies to dispatch — up to the point defined in the MEL preamble (commonly the start of the flight or the take-off roll). After that point, a failure is handled with the QRH / FCOM / AOM non-normal procedures, and the commander decides on continuation using the AFM and good airmanship.

Key takeaways

  • MMEL from the manufacturer; MEL from the operator — never less restrictive.
  • Required equipment not in the MEL must work.
  • CDL covers missing external parts, often with performance penalties.
  • After the point defined in the preamble, use the QRH, not the MEL.

Knowledge check

Can an operator's MEL be less restrictive than the MMEL?

  1. Yes
  2. No
  3. Only for short flights
  4. Only with captain's approval

Answer: B. The MEL must not be less restrictive than the MMEL.

Module 1 · MEL Principles

Lesson 02: Reading an MEL Item

Lesson 2 of 4 · about 7 min

Learning objectives

  • Read the columns and conditions of an MEL item.
  • Apply the rectification interval categories.
  • Apply (M) and (O) procedures and placards.

The columns

ColumnMeaning
System / item (ATA chapter)The equipment, by ATA reference (e.g. 28 fuel, 73 engine fuel and control)
Rectification interval categoryThe time allowed to repair the item (A, B, C or D)
Number installedHow many are fitted
Number required for dispatchHow many must work
Remarks or exceptionsThe conditions, limitations and procedures for dispatch

Rectification interval categories

CategoryInterval
ANo standard interval — as stated in the remarks of the item
B3 consecutive calendar days, excluding the day of discovery
C10 consecutive calendar days, excluding the day of discovery
D120 consecutive calendar days, excluding the day of discovery

A one-time extension of categories B, C or D may be allowed under a procedure approved by the authority — never by the crew.

(M), (O) and placards

  • (M) — a maintenance procedure must be performed before dispatch (e.g. deactivating or securing the item).
  • (O) — an operational procedure must be applied by the crew (e.g. a performance penalty, a fuel requirement, a procedure in flight).
  • The inoperative item must be placarded and recorded in the technical log.
Example — B737-800 MEL 28-01-03-01 (from the company LOFT)

(M)(O) One main tank aft boost pump may be inoperative provided that: (a) both main tank forward pumps operate normally; (b) at the start of take-off, fuel in the associated tank is not less than 7,500 lb (3,402 kg); (c) a minimum of 2,500 lb (1,134 kg) is maintained in the associated tank; and (d) the boost pump is deactivated. — Training example only; always use the current MEL.

Key takeaways

  • Read every column: category, installed, required, remarks.
  • Categories: A as stated, B 3 days, C 10 days, D 120 days (excluding day of discovery).
  • (M) maintenance and (O) operational procedures; placard and log the item.

Knowledge check

What is the rectification interval for a category C item?

  1. 3 days
  2. 10 calendar days excluding the day of discovery
  3. 120 days
  4. As stated in the remarks

Answer: B. Category C: 10 consecutive calendar days, excluding the day of discovery.

Module 2 · Using the MEL

Lesson 03: Using the MEL for Dispatch

Lesson 3 of 4 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Apply the MEL dispatch process as a crew.
  • Combine several MEL items correctly.
  • Recognise items that affect specific approvals and performance.

The dispatch process

  1. Read the technical log: open defects and deferred items.
  2. Find each item in the MEL and read the preamble and definitions if in doubt.
  3. Check the number required, the category and expiry date, and the remarks.
  4. Confirm that the (M) procedure has been completed and the placard fitted.
  5. Apply the (O) procedure: performance penalties, fuel, flight planning restrictions, in-flight actions.
  6. Brief the crew on the effect of the item.

Several items together

The MEL items are written independently. When several items are inoperative, the commander must consider their combined effect — some combinations are not acceptable even if each item alone is. Check the MEL preamble guidance on multiple items.

Specific approvals and performance

  • The MEL contains the dispatch requirements for systems that need a specific approval or capability: RVSM, RNAV/PBN, LVO, and others. An item can remove a capability (for example: no RVSM, no CAT II).
  • Many items carry performance penalties — more critical on hot days and short runways.
  • Some items restrict operation in icing conditions or at night.
Commander's decision

Dispatch under the MEL is allowed, not obligatory. The commander may refuse an aircraft if, in the actual conditions, the combination of items is unsafe.

Key takeaways

  • Tech log → MEL item → category and expiry → (M) done and placard → (O) applied → brief.
  • Consider the combined effect of several items.
  • MEL items can remove RVSM, PBN or LVO capability and add performance penalties.

Knowledge check

Two MEL items are each acceptable alone. What must the commander consider?

  1. Nothing more
  2. Their combined effect on safety in the actual conditions
  3. Only the item with the longer interval
  4. Only the newer item

Answer: B. The combined effect of multiple items must be considered.

Module 2 · Using the MEL

Lesson 04: Failures After Dispatch and Good Practice

Lesson 4 of 4 · about 5 min

Learning objectives

  • Handle failures after the MEL no longer applies.
  • Report defects correctly.
  • Avoid common MEL errors.

Failures after dispatch

  • After the point defined in the MEL preamble, use the QRH / FCOM / AOM non-normal procedures.
  • The decision to continue, return or divert is taken by the commander, based on the AFM limitations, the procedures and the situation — the MEL can help to assess the consequences, but does not authorise continuation by itself.
  • Record every defect in the technical log after the flight.

Common errors

  • Dispatching with an expired rectification interval.
  • Forgetting the (O) procedure — for example a fuel or performance penalty.
  • Misreading “number required”.
  • Not checking the effect on RVSM, PBN or LVO capability.
  • Not informing the other crew member of the restrictions.
Company MEL

Always use the current Domestic Airlines MEL for your fleet (e.g. MEL Ed.02) and its preamble. This course explains the principles; the MEL decides.

Key takeaways

  • After the MEL point, use QRH / FCOM / AOM and commander's judgment.
  • Record every defect in the technical log.
  • Watch expiry dates, (O) procedures, number required and capability effects.

Knowledge check

A system fails during the climb. Which document do you use first?

  1. The MEL
  2. The QRH / FCOM / AOM non-normal procedure
  3. The CDL
  4. The OM D

Answer: B. After dispatch, failures are handled with the non-normal procedures.
