Domestic Airlines — Inoperative Ground Equipment
Lesson 1: Why Ground Equipment Matters

Learning objectives:
- Explain why published minima assume serviceable ground equipment.
- Identify how failures are notified.
- Describe the possible effects on an approach.

Minima assume serviceable equipment

Aerodrome operating minima are established for a runway with its navigation aids, lighting and RVR equipment serviceable (EASA CAT.OP.MPA.110; ICAO Doc 9365). When an item fails or is downgraded, the minima may have to be increased , or the approach may not be available .

How you find out

NOTAM — planned or known outages (e.g. ILS glide path unserviceable, approach lights out of service).

ATIS and ATC — recent failures.

Flight deck indications — loss of an ILS signal, flags, or visible lighting failures.

Possible effects

No effect — for example a failure that has an equivalent replacement (a marker replaced by a DME distance or another fix).

Higher minima — for example an increased RVR when approach lights are out.

Downgrade — for example from CAT II to CAT I, or from a 3D to a 2D approach (ILS to LOC only).

Approach not available .

Company table The effect of each failed or downgraded item on the minima is given in the “failed or downgraded equipment” table of the Domestic Airlines OM A . Use that table — this course explains how it works, not its values.

Key takeaways:
- Minima assume serviceable ground aids, lighting and RVR equipment.
- Failures come from NOTAM, ATIS / ATC or flight deck indications.
- Effects: none, higher minima, downgrade, or no approach.
