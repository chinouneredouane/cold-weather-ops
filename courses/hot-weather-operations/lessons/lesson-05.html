Lesson 05: Summer Flight Safety Focus Items

Learning objectives

  • Review the flight safety events selected for this season.
  • Apply the correct technique to prevent each event.
  • Report events through the safety reporting system.

The training department has selected the following flight safety items for discussion with the TRI/TRE:

EventWhy it mattersPrevention
High taxi speedHot brakes, reduced margins on turns and runway crossings, passenger injury.Respect the company taxi speeds; use idle thrust and brief, firm braking applications rather than riding the brakes.
Late or long flareEach second of float at high ground speed uses a large part of the runway and increases the landing distance.Fly a stabilised approach to the aiming point; flare at the normal height; go around if a long landing develops.
Incorrect flap setting, ZFW or fuel entriesWrong take-off speeds and trim — tail strike or degraded take-off performance.Independent cross-check of performance data, FMS / performance entries and the load sheet by both pilots.
Hard landingStructural damage, injuries; often after an unstable approach or late flare.Stabilised approach; correct flare; report any suspected hard landing in the technical log.
Bounced landingRisk of tail strike, nose gear damage or a second hard landing.Apply the company bounced landing recovery technique; go around if in doubt.
Use of reverse and APUCovered by the company reminder note (note de rappel).Apply the reminder note exactly as published.

Stabilised approach, normal flare — go around if a long landing develops.

Safety notes and reporting

  • Read and discuss the latest company safety notes before the simulator session.
  • Report events through the safety reporting system. Reports are used to improve training and procedures, under a just culture.

Key takeaways

  • Moderate taxi speeds protect brakes and people.
  • A late or long flare at high ground speed eats runway — go around if needed.
  • Cross-check flap, ZFW and fuel entries.
  • Apply the bounced and hard landing techniques; report every event.
