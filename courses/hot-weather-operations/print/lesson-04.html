Module 2 · Weather, Safety and Preparation

Lesson 04: Sand, Dust and Convective Weather

Lesson 4 of 6 · about 7 min

Learning objectives

  • Recognise the hazards of dust, sand and drifting sand.
  • Apply precautions on the ground and in flight.
  • Manage convective weather and gusty winds in summer.

Dust and sand

  • Watch the reports for DU (dust), SA (sand), DRSA (low drifting sand), BLSA (blowing sand), SS / DS (sandstorm / duststorm). Visibility can fall very quickly.
  • During the walk-around in dusty conditions, check pitot probes, static ports, engine inlets, APU inlet and air conditioning inlets. Fit covers when the aircraft is parked.
  • Minimise engine and APU running and high thrust on sand-covered surfaces to limit ingestion. Use the procedure for taxi and take-off on contaminated or sandy surfaces if published.
  • Sand on the runway reduces braking — treat it like a contaminant and report braking action if worse than expected.

Convective weather and wind

  • Summer thunderstorms (CB, TCU) bring gusts, windshear, turbulence and sudden dust storms. Avoid cells by a wide margin.
  • Gusty winds — for example a reported wind of 18 kt gusting 28 kt — require a careful check of the company crosswind limits and the appropriate speed additives.
  • Thermal turbulence close to the ground makes approaches less stable — be ready to go around.
  • Dust devils and localised strong winds can affect take-off and landing.

Go-around in hot weather

Climb performance is lower in hot, high conditions. Brief the missed approach, the climb gradient and any obstacles. Know the go-around technique for your type, including engine-out, and practise it in the simulator in hot weather conditions.

Key takeaways

  • DU, SA, DRSA, SS/DS: visibility can collapse — watch the trend.
  • Protect inlets and probes; minimise ground running and high thrust on sand.
  • Summer CB and gusts: wide avoidance, check crosswind limits and speed additives.
  • Lower go-around performance in hot, high conditions — brief it.

Knowledge check

What does DRSA in a METAR mean?

  1. Dry runway, sand removed
  2. Low drifting sand
  3. Drizzle and sand
  4. Dust storm

Answer: B. DRSA = low drifting sand.
