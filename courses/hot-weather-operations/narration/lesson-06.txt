Domestic Airlines — Hot Weather Operations
Lesson 6: MEL, Dispatch and the Summer Simulator Session

Learning objectives:
- Apply the MEL and dispatch criteria for a summer flight.
- Prepare the summer recurrent simulator session.
- Combine hot weather, performance and LOFT topics.

MEL and dispatch

Check every MEL item for its effect on performance — some items impose mass penalties or limitations that matter more in hot weather.

Apply the (M) and (O) procedures, the placards and any dispatch conditions.

The MEL also contains the dispatch requirements for systems that need a specific approval — RVSM, RNAV/PBN and LVO.

The summer recurrent programme

Day 1 — ground refresher : engine and APU operation and limitations in hot weather, hot weather procedures, fuel, APU start on battery, bleeds-off take-off and landing, QRH performance, fuel system checks, RVSM, MEL and dispatch.

Days 2 and 3 — simulator : LOFT sectors to and from hot, high southern aerodromes with dust and drifting sand, MEL dispatch items, go-arounds and engine failures in hot weather, an RNAV approach briefing including temperature effects, and an LVO approach.

Mandatory exercises : upset recovery, windshear identification and escape, TCAS, EGPWS and pilot incapacitation — performed according to company procedures and DACM recommendations.

Preparing

Prepare the performance dispatch from the Jetplan in the briefing room and discuss it as a crew.

Review the courses in this academy: CFIT and EGPWS, RVSM, Upset Prevention and Recovery.

Key takeaways:
- Check MEL items for performance penalties — critical in hot weather.
- Summer recurrent: ground refresher day, then two simulator days with LOFT and mandatory exercises.
- Prepare performance and review the related academy courses before the session.
