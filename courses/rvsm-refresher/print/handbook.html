Module 1 · RVSM Operations

Lesson 01: RVSM Airspace, Approval and Equipment

Lesson 1 of 6 · about 7 min

Learning objectives

  • Define RVSM airspace and its benefits.
  • List the equipment required for RVSM operations.
  • State which Domestic Airlines fleets are RVSM approved.

What is RVSM?

Reduced Vertical Separation Minimum (RVSM) airspace is any airspace or route between FL290 and FL410 inclusive where aircraft are separated vertically by 1,000 ft (300 m) instead of 2,000 ft (600 m).

The reduction adds six flight levels — FL300, FL320, FL340, FL360, FL380 and FL400 — giving significant benefits in fuel economy and en-route airspace capacity.

Approval

ICAO requires States to approve operators and aircraft for RVSM and to establish initial and recurrent training programmes for flight crews (ICAO Doc 9574, Manual on a 300 m (1 000 ft) Vertical Separation Minimum; Annex 6). In Europe this is a specific approval under EASA Part-SPA (SPA.RVSM). Both the aircraft (airworthiness) and the operator (procedures and training) must be approved.

FleetRVSMNotes
Boeing 737-800✅ RVSM approvedAll RVSM procedures in this course apply. Check the MEL for RVSM-related items before every flight.
Dash 8-Q400— Not applicableOperates below RVSM airspace (maximum operating altitude below FL290). This course gives awareness of RVSM traffic, phraseology and TCAS.
Dash 8-Q200— Not applicableOperates below RVSM airspace. Awareness only, as for the Q400.

Required equipment

  • Two independent altitude measurement systems;
  • one SSR transponder with altitude reporting, connected to the altitude measurement system in use for height-keeping;
  • an altitude alerting system;
  • an automatic altitude control system (autopilot).

The minimum aircraft system performance specification (MASPS) sets tight limits on altimetry system error — of the order of 80 ft — and requires the automatic altitude control to hold the cleared level within about ±65 ft in normal conditions; altitude excursions of more than about 200 ft must not occur in level flight. Exact values are in your AFM RVSM statement.

Key takeaways

  • RVSM: FL290–FL410 inclusive, 1,000 ft separation, six extra levels.
  • Approval required for both aircraft and operator (ICAO Doc 9574, EASA SPA.RVSM).
  • Equipment: two altimetry systems, transponder, altitude alerter, autopilot with altitude hold.
  • B737-800: RVSM approved. Q400 and Q200: operate below RVSM airspace.

Knowledge check

Answer: B. RVSM airspace extends from FL290 to FL410 inclusive.

Module 1 · RVSM Operations

Lesson 02: Flight Planning and Pre-flight

Lesson 2 of 6 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Complete the flight plan correctly for an RVSM flight.
  • Perform the RVSM pre-flight checks.
  • Apply the MEL to RVSM equipment.

Flight planning

  • In the ICAO flight plan, insert the letter “W” in item 10 to indicate RVSM approval (and in a repetitive flight plan, the letter “Q” where required by the procedure).
  • Verify that the aircraft is RVSM compliant and that no RVSM-related item is open in the technical log.
  • Check forecast and reported weather — especially severe turbulence and mountain wave activity at cruise levels.
  • Check the MEL: the MEL contains the dispatch requirements for systems that need a specific approval, such as RVSM, RNAV and LVO.
  • Check any RVSM operating limitations in the AFM.

Pre-flight at the aircraft

  • Review the maintenance logs and forms for the serviceability of RVSM equipment.
  • During the exterior inspection, check the static ports and the condition of the fuselage skin around the pitot-static sources. Damage, dents or residue near the static ports can cause altimetry errors.
  • On the ground, compare the altimeters with the known aerodrome elevation. The difference must not exceed the limit in your FCOM (typically about 75 ft).
  • Before take-off, confirm that the RVSM equipment is operative.
Good practice

Brief the RVSM status in the departure briefing: equipment state, MEL items, expected cruise level and any turbulence or mountain wave forecast.

Key takeaways

  • Flight plan item 10: “W” for RVSM approval.
  • Check MEL, weather and technical log before an RVSM flight.
  • Inspect static ports; compare altimeters with aerodrome elevation (FCOM limit, typically 75 ft).

Knowledge check

Which letter in the ICAO flight plan indicates RVSM approval?

  1. R
  2. W
  3. Y
  4. Z

Answer: B. The letter W in item 10 indicates RVSM approval.

Module 1 · RVSM Operations

Lesson 03: Entering and Flying in RVSM Airspace

Lesson 3 of 6 · about 7 min

Learning objectives

  • List the equipment that must be operating before entering RVSM airspace.
  • Apply the en-route height-keeping procedures.
  • Perform and record the altimeter cross-checks.

Before entering RVSM airspace

The following equipment must be operating normally:

  • two primary altitude measurement systems;
  • one automatic altitude control system;
  • one altitude alerting device;
  • the transponder.

If any of this equipment fails before entry, request a new clearance to avoid RVSM airspace, or follow the established contingency procedures.

En-route procedures

  • Fly at the cleared flight level (CFL). Make sure every ATC clearance is fully understood, read back and cross-checked by both pilots.
  • Except in an emergency, do not intentionally leave the CFL without an ATC clearance.
  • Use the automatic altitude capture for level changes. Overshoots and undershoots should not exceed 150 ft.
  • Reduce the vertical rate to less than 1,500 ft/min in the last 1,000 ft before the cleared level, especially when traffic is at the adjacent level (see Lesson 5).
  • In cruise, fly with the automatic altitude control engaged, except when the aircraft must be re-trimmed or in turbulence where the procedure requires otherwise.

Altimeter cross-checks

  • At intervals of about one hour, cross-check the primary and standby altimeters.
  • The two primary altimeters must agree within 200 ft.
  • Record at least the initial cross-check — on the operational flight plan (Jetplan).
  • Normally, the altimetry system controlling the autopilot should also be the source for the transponder's altitude reporting. If not, report the system as defective and notify ATC.

Key takeaways

  • Before entry: two altimetry systems, autopilot altitude hold, altitude alerter, transponder.
  • Fly the CFL with altitude hold engaged; overshoot or undershoot less than 150 ft.
  • Less than 1,500 ft/min in the last 1,000 ft before the cleared level.
  • About every hour: primary altimeters within 200 ft; record the initial check.

Knowledge check

What is the maximum difference allowed between the two primary altimeters in RVSM airspace?

  1. 75 ft
  2. 150 ft
  3. 200 ft
  4. 300 ft

Answer: C. The primary altimeters must agree within 200 ft.

Module 2 · Contingencies, ACAS II and Phraseology

Lesson 04: Contingencies and Special Situations

Lesson 4 of 6 · about 9 min

Learning objectives

  • Recognise the contingencies that must be reported to ATC.
  • Apply the pilot actions for equipment failures, turbulence, wake and mountain waves.
  • Explain the 2,000 ft separation cases.

Tell ATC as soon as possible

An in-flight contingency is any unforeseen circumstance that affects the ability to meet the RVSM height-keeping requirements. Inform ATC as soon as possible of:

  • failure of the automatic altitude control system;
  • loss of redundancy of the primary altimetry systems, or loss of all primary altimeters;
  • loss of thrust requiring a descent;
  • failure of any other equipment affecting height-keeping;
  • an encounter with greater than moderate turbulence.

Obtain a revised clearance before deviating from the cleared route or level whenever possible. If that is not possible, obtain it as soon as possible afterwards. If you cannot contact ATC, follow the regional contingency procedures.

When the aircraft no longer meets RVSM requirements, ATC treats it as non-RVSM and applies 2,000 ft vertical separation or a horizontal separation minimum.

Scenarios

ScenarioPilot actions
Loss of automatic altitude control, altitude alerter or all primary altimetersTell ATC: “Unable RVSM due equipment.” Request a clearance out of RVSM airspace unless the operational situation dictates otherwise.
Only one primary altimeter remainsCross-check it with the standby altimeter. Notify ATC of operation with a single primary altimeter. If its accuracy cannot be confirmed, act as for the loss of all primary altimeters.
Transponder failureContact ATC and request authority to continue at the cleared level. Comply with any revised clearance.
Severe turbulenceTell ATC: “Unable RVSM due turbulence.” Request a vector clear of traffic if not offered, and a level change or re-route if desired. ATC applies horizontal separation or 2,000 ft.
Wake turbulenceUsually moderate or less at RVSM levels; the wind decides the risk. Request a vector, a level change or a lateral offset (where authorised).
Mountain wave activityReport it to ATC. If traffic is called at an adjacent level, request a vector to avoid merging targets; request a level change or re-route if desired.

Special cases

  • Communication failure: ICAO procedures apply 2,000 ft vertical separation between the aircraft with the failure and other aircraft in RVSM airspace, unless horizontal separation exists.
  • RVSM suspension: in a portion of airspace where RVSM is suspended (for example in widespread severe turbulence), 2,000 ft separation is applied to all aircraft.
  • State aircraft: non-RVSM-approved State (e.g. military) aircraft may be accommodated; ATC applies 2,000 ft separation to them.
  • Non-RVSM aircraft climbing or descending through RVSM levels to or from FL430 and above are handled workload permitting, and must be able to climb or descend continuously at a normal rate.
  • Regional differences: RVSM is global, but contingency procedures can differ between regions. The commander must know the procedures of the region being flown.

Key takeaways

  • Report to ATC: autopilot altitude hold, altimetry, thrust or height-keeping failures, and turbulence greater than moderate.
  • Phrases: “Unable RVSM due equipment” / “Unable RVSM due turbulence”.
  • Non-RVSM status, communication failure and RVSM suspension mean 2,000 ft separation.
  • Know the regional contingency procedures.

Knowledge check

  3. “Mayday, mayday, mayday”

Module 2 · Contingencies, ACAS II and Phraseology

Lesson 05: TCAS / ACAS II in RVSM Airspace

Lesson 5 of 6 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Describe the ACAS II version 7.1 changes.
  • Apply procedures that avoid nuisance resolution advisories at adjacent levels.
  • Respond correctly to a resolution advisory.

ACAS II version 7.1

ACAS II (TCAS II) version 7.1 is the standard required by ICAO and EASA, replacing version 7.0. The main operational change for crews:

  • The “ADJUST VERTICAL SPEED, ADJUST” resolution advisory has been replaced by “LEVEL OFF, LEVEL OFF”, which always requires the vertical rate to be reduced to 0 ft/min.
  • The reversal logic has been improved, so that an RA can reverse when the other aircraft does not follow its own RA.

Operate TCAS in TA/RA mode during RVSM operations, including in the transition areas.

Avoiding nuisance RAs at adjacent levels

With only 1,000 ft between aircraft, a high vertical rate close to the cleared level can trigger an RA in an aircraft at the adjacent level. ICAO procedures therefore recommend that when a pilot is aware of traffic at an adjacent level (for example from the traffic display), the aircraft climbs or descends at less than 1,500 ft/min throughout the last 1,000 ft before the assigned level — especially with the autopilot engaged.

Responding to an RA

  • Follow the RA promptly and accurately, even if it conflicts with an ATC clearance or instruction. Never manoeuvre in the opposite sense to an RA.
  • Tell ATC as soon as possible: “(call sign), TCAS RA.”
  • When clear: “(call sign), clear of conflict, returning to (assigned clearance)” — then return promptly to the clearance.
  • File the company report after any RA.
Remember

A TCAS RA takes precedence over ATC instructions. Following an ATC instruction against an RA has caused mid-air collisions.

Key takeaways

  • ACAS II v7.1: “LEVEL OFF, LEVEL OFF” replaces “ADJUST VERTICAL SPEED”; improved reversal logic.
  • TA/RA mode in RVSM airspace.
  • Less than 1,500 ft/min in the last 1,000 ft when traffic is at an adjacent level.
  • Always follow the RA, then inform ATC.

Knowledge check

What does the ACAS II 7.1 resolution advisory “LEVEL OFF, LEVEL OFF” require?

  1. Reduce vertical speed by half
  2. Reduce vertical speed to 0 ft/min
  3. Climb at 1,500 ft/min
  4. Return to the ATC clearance

Answer: B. “LEVEL OFF, LEVEL OFF” always requires the vertical rate to be reduced to 0 ft/min.

Lesson 06: Phraseology, Reporting and Crew Knowledge

Lesson 6 of 6 · about 6 min

Learning objectives

  • Use the standard RVSM phraseology.
  • Complete the post-flight entries and reports.
  • Review the RVSM knowledge items required for recurrent training.

Standard phraseology

CircumstancePhraseology
ATC asks the RVSM status of a flight“Confirm RVSM approved.”
Pilot: the flight is RVSM approved“Affirm RVSM.”
Pilot: the flight is not RVSM approved“Negative RVSM.”
Pilot of a State aircraft that is not RVSM approved“Negative RVSM, State aircraft.”
ATC denies entry to RVSM airspace“Unable clearance into RVSM airspace, maintain (or descend to, or climb to) flight level …”
Pilot: weather prevents height-keeping (e.g. severe turbulence)“Unable RVSM due turbulence.”
Pilot: equipment degraded below RVSM requirements“Unable RVSM due equipment.”
ATC asks to be told when RVSM can be resumed“Report able to resume RVSM.”
Pilot: able to resume after a contingency“Ready to resume RVSM.”

After the flight

  • Enter in the technical log any problem with altitude-keeping, transponder failures, or altimeter differences greater than 200 ft.
  • File the altitude deviation report for any deviation of 300 ft or more from the cleared level.
  • File the wake turbulence encounter report after any significant wake encounter.

What every crew member must know (recurrent training items)

  1. Airworthiness documents and the aircraft's RVSM approval;
  2. operating practices and procedures, the Operations Manual and checklists;
  3. the MEL items for RVSM;
  4. standard ATC phraseology and cross-checking of clearances;
  5. use of the standby altimeter in contingencies;
  6. problems of visual perception of other aircraft only 1,000 ft away;
  7. characteristics of the altitude capture system;
  8. the relationship between altimetry, automatic altitude control and transponder in normal and abnormal conditions;
  9. any airframe operating restrictions for RVSM.

Key takeaways

  • Know the standard phrases: Affirm / Negative RVSM, Unable RVSM due turbulence / equipment, Ready to resume RVSM.
  • Technical log: altimeter differences over 200 ft, altitude-keeping or transponder problems.
  • Report altitude deviations of 300 ft or more, and wake turbulence encounters.
