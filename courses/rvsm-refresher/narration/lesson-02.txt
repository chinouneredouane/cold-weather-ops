Domestic Airlines — RVSM Refresher
Lesson 2: Flight Planning and Pre-flight

Learning objectives:
- Complete the flight plan correctly for an RVSM flight.
- Perform the RVSM pre-flight checks.
- Apply the MEL to RVSM equipment.

Flight planning

In the ICAO flight plan, insert the letter “W” in item 10 to indicate RVSM approval (and in a repetitive flight plan, the letter “Q” where required by the procedure).

Verify that the aircraft is RVSM compliant and that no RVSM-related item is open in the technical log.

Check forecast and reported weather — especially severe turbulence and mountain wave activity at cruise levels.

Check the MEL : the MEL contains the dispatch requirements for systems that need a specific approval, such as RVSM, RNAV and LVO.

Check any RVSM operating limitations in the AFM.

Pre-flight at the aircraft

Review the maintenance logs and forms for the serviceability of RVSM equipment.

During the exterior inspection, check the static ports and the condition of the fuselage skin around the pitot-static sources. Damage, dents or residue near the static ports can cause altimetry errors.

On the ground, compare the altimeters with the known aerodrome elevation. The difference must not exceed the limit in your FCOM (typically about 75 ft ).

Before take-off, confirm that the RVSM equipment is operative.

Good practice Brief the RVSM status in the departure briefing: equipment state, MEL items, expected cruise level and any turbulence or mountain wave forecast.

Key takeaways:
- Flight plan item 10: “W” for RVSM approval.
- Check MEL, weather and technical log before an RVSM flight.
- Inspect static ports; compare altimeters with aerodrome elevation (FCOM limit, typically 75 ft).
